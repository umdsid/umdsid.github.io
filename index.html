Research

Four threads that define my current work.

Kidney Structure as Graph

Converting 3D fluorescence light-sheet microscopy into anatomically grounded graphs of glomeruli, vessels, nerves, and ducts, then quantifying multiscale organization across the tissue.

Spatial Biology Pipelines

Building end-to-end imaging pipelines for 3D segmentation, registration, feature extraction, and downstream network analysis aligned with large atlas efforts such as HuBMAP.

Neural Criticality

Studying avalanche statistics and crackling-noise behavior in whole-brain calcium imaging, with models that incorporate asymmetry, probability currents, and plasticity.

Inference Far from Equilibrium

Developing latent-variable and optimal-transport methods for inference and control in asymmetric nonequilibrium networks where time-ordered observations may be incomplete or absent.

Selected Contributions

Work shaped by both theory and high-dimensional data.

Spatial Atlas

3D multiscalar mapping of the human kidney

Contributed to a lifespan-scale neurovascular-nephron connectivity map of the human kidney, linking 3D anatomy to measurable mesoscale organization.

Tooling

NetTracer3D for network analysis of 3D spatial maps

Helped develop a toolchain for transforming rich spatial tissue maps into analyzable graphs for structural and functional interpretation.

Neuroscience

Zebrafish sleep and sleep-deprivation analysis

Built preprocessing and event-extraction workflows for whole-brain calcium imaging and used them to study avalanche statistics in sleep-related neural activity.

Theory

Inference and control in asymmetric networks

Developed thesis and preprint work on inverse problems, latent-variable likelihood methods, and optimal driving between nonequilibrium steady states of unequal dimensions.
